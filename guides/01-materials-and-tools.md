The 5/8 spruce nest is tight: it needs a true 48 x 96 sheet, so measure the sheet in
the store. The MDF must be a true 49 x 97; a 48 x 96 sheet breaks nest B in guide 2
(nest A still works). Sight every spruce sheet along its length for bow before it goes
in the car; a cupped sheet makes a twisted tall box. Keep MDF dry.

No legs, no kick clips, no connector screws: nothing is boxed together any more.

Why half overlay everywhere: the model gives every hinge edge a 1/4 gap to the wall
or to the tall cabinet. On a 5/8 side that is a 3/8 (9.5 mm) cover, which is a half
overlay hinge on a 0 mm plate. A full overlay hinge (16 mm cover) would put the tall
door's edge against the wall. Door 1 gets the same hinge on a 5/8 packer (guide 4).
If you buy full overlay hinges instead, pair them with 6 mm plates on those doors.

The target was under CA$500. At these unit prices it is not reachable with paint and
glue included: the sheet goods alone are 282 and the fixed items (paint, screws, pins,
glue, filler) are about 200. It gets close if you already own the Forstner, glue and
filler (~540) and the 2x2 price is 3 not 4. Guide 8's 541 for this option counted
fewer screw types and no glue or filler.

## Tools you have that this build uses

Circular saw with a straightedge guide, miter saw, two drills, clamps (at least four
24" bar clamps and two 36" or longer), tape, 4' level, square, chisels, hand saw,
block plane, sanding block, stud finder.

## Tools to borrow or improvise

- **Straightedge guide for the circular saw**: built in guide 2 from the 1/2 MDF and
  hardboard offcuts.
- **Brad nailer**: optional. The back can be screwed.
- **Laser level or a string line**: the ladder is only as straight as its rails; a
  string line along the rail face is enough.
- **Right-angle drill attachment**: helps under the TV bottoms and inside the bays.
