# 7. Top slab

Half a day, plus drying. The slab is two pieces of 1/2 MDF, 16 deep, that become one
after filling and painting. It sits on the three rails, the five divider tops and
the six top-front stringers, all on line E (19-7/16), and brings the run to 15 high.

## Fit

1. Left piece 90-53/64 is cut to length already: tall cabinet's right side to the
   centre of divider 3 (90-13/16 from the tall cabinet). Right piece: measure from
   the centre of divider 3 to the right wall at the top (nominal 90-31/64) and cut
   the piece 1/2 long for the scribe. Cut the joint ends of both pieces square and
   clean on the same saw setting, tape on the line.
2. Dry fit. The joint lands over the middle of divider 3, so it is supported along
   its full depth. Front edge flush with the door faces (16 from the wall); rear edge
   to the wall. If the wall is not straight, scribe the rear edge with a block plane
   rather than leaving a gap at the front. Trim the right piece to length at the
   wall last.

## Fix

3. Glue: a bead along every divider top, every top-front stringer and the top of the
   three rails. The rails carry the slab at the rear and glue is the only fixing
   there; a 4" screw up through a 2x4 is not.
4. Screws from below, through the top-front stringers only: counterbore 1/2 dia,
   7/8 deep up into each stringer from below, then a #8 x 1" screw through the
   remaining 5/8 of stringer into the slab. That is 3/8 of bite in a 1/2 slab; test
   one on the offcut first, anything longer comes through the top. Three per
   stringer, 1" back from the front edge. A right-angle attachment or a stubby
   driver fits in the 12-1/2 bay.
5. Dividers: if a divider top will not pull up tight under the slab on glue alone,
   one #6 x 1-5/8 toe-screwed from the divider's face 1" below its top edge, angled
   up into the slab at 45°, on the hinge-plate side where the door hides it.
6. At the joint: glue the two end faces, pull the pieces together, and screw both
   pieces through the bay 3 and bay 4 stringers within 3" of the joint. Clamp a
   straight 2x4 offcut across the joint on top while it dries so both faces stay
   coplanar. Wipe squeeze-out.

## Hide the seam

7. Next day, fill the seam with 2-part filler, spread with a wide spreader, slightly
   proud. Fill any dents and the kick screw heads while it is mixed.
8. Sand flat with 220 on a block, running your hand across the joint with a torch
   held low. If you can feel it, fill again.
9. Prime the whole top face and front edge (the pieces came from guide 5 primed;
   this coat ties the seam in). Sand 220. Second primer coat on the seam area if you
   can still see a line.
10. Two colour coats, same black eggshell as the doors. Brush the front faces of the
    top-front stringers while the brush is wet; they show in the 1/8 reveal. Let it
    cure a week before loading it.

## If the seam shows later

MDF moves little, but a hairline can open after a season. Fill with paintable caulk,
wipe flush, touch up with a small brush. Eggshell makes this invisible; gloss does
not.
